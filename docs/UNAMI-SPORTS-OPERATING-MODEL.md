# Unami Sports

## Executive summary

**Unami Sports is Unami Foundation’s community-sport enablement programme.** It helps grassroots teams build the practical capability, relationships and digital infrastructure needed to manage their own sporting affairs and public identity.

The programme is designed around a **community sports operating partnership**: a team remains in charge of its club life; Unami Foundation acts as a non-profit steward and operating partner, donating and maintaining useful digital infrastructure, training team administrators, providing technical support, and coordinating relationships with stakeholders where agreed.

The technology is an enabling asset—not the programme, the organisation, or the source of a team’s authority. A website or CMS alone cannot create a functioning club, keep young people safe, secure funding, establish football affiliation, or make an organisation sustainable. Those outcomes depend on community leadership, accountable governance, appropriate safeguarding, practical training, reliable operations and relationships that have clear boundaries.

Unami Stars is the illustrative pilot: a **fictional** youth football team situated in the rural South African community named in the brief, Emndozо / Emndozo, KwaGucingo. It demonstrates how the model could support a community team. It is not evidence of a registered club, real roster, league affiliation, scheduled fixtures, confirmed partners, funding, or delivered programme outcomes. Its content must be verified with the community before being presented as factual.

This document explains the intended service, roles, digital system, governance approach, pilot lifecycle, risks, customisation opportunities and the decisions needed before operational or public launch. It is a product and operating-model document, **not legal advice, a club constitution, a safeguarding policy, a funding commitment or proof of regulatory compliance**.

## 1. Recommended ontology and language

The words used to describe Unami matter. “Platform” sounds like a software product and can obscure the people, responsibilities and ongoing service required to make the technology useful. A more accurate description is a **non-profit-led community sport enablement programme**, delivered through an **operating partnership** and supported by shared digital infrastructure.

### The naming hierarchy

| Layer                      | Recommended term                                              | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Legal designation          | **Non-profit organisation**; use **NPC** only when verified   | “Non-profit” describes the intended organisational character. “NPC” is a specific South African company form and should be used as a legal claim only after checking the organisation’s registration and governing records. |
| Operating approach         | **Social-enterprise operating model**                         | A description of how Unami seeks to create social value and sustain useful services. It is not, by itself, a legal entity type, tax status, registration, or guarantee that income or services exist.                       |

It does not make the Foundation the club’s governing body or replace its committee.                                                   |

### Public-description recommendation

> **Unami Sports is Unami Foundation’s community-sport enablement programme. Through a community sports operating partnership, Unami Foundation donates and supports practical digital infrastructure while grassroots teams retain responsibility for their own sporting decisions, local coordination, identity and affairs. The programme aims to build local capability, strengthen stakeholder relationships and support continuity—not dependency.**

Until legal status is confirmed, describe the organisation as “Unami Foundation” or “a non-profit organisation” only if that description is accurate and approved by its leadership. Say **“Unami Foundation NPC” only after verifying the registered legal name and company number**. Describe Unami Sports as a social-enterprise model or programme only where that accurately reflects approved organisational plans and actual operating arrangements.

### Terms to avoid or qualify

- Avoid describing Unami Sports as a league, governing body, club owner, player agent, funder, academy, or football authority unless that role is formally established.
- Avoid saying the programme “empowers” teams as an outcome claim without evidence. Prefer “is designed to support team-led management and local capability”.
- Avoid “free forever”. A donated initial platform does not remove hosting, subscriptions, connectivity, support labour, security, maintenance or future migration costs.
- Avoid implying that sample text, sample team records or workflow labels are approved policies or technical access controls.
- Avoid presenting illustrative names, results, affiliations, achievements, sponsors or testimonials as real.

## 2. Mission, value proposition and theory of change

### Programme purpose

Unami Sports seeks to help community-rooted teams spend less effort struggling with fragmented administration and more effort organising the sport and community activity they value. The service combines technology with human support and practical operating knowledge.

### The problem the programme is designed to address

Grassroots organisations may rely on a small number of unpaid volunteers, informal communication channels, paper records, personal devices and short-lived partner support. Rural settings can add distance, variable connectivity, transport constraints, limited equipment and fewer nearby service providers. These are design considerations, **not claims that every named community experiences every barrier**.

A website alone does not resolve those conditions. A useful response should:

1. Start with how a team actually works, what it wants to control, and what support it has asked for.
2. Provide a low-friction place to communicate approved information and coordinate routine tasks.
3. Train more than one local administrator where practical, with plain-language guides and a handover process.
4. Preserve the team’s authority over its identity, sporting choices and local affairs.
5. Give the Foundation a transparent way to coordinate stakeholder commitments, platform stewardship and requested changes.
6. Make privacy, youth safeguarding, consent and publication review part of the operating workflow.
7. Plan for interruption, staff/volunteer change, funding change, data export and eventual transition.

### Intended theory of change

| Inputs                                                                                                                         | Activities                                                                                                                                                                    | Near-term outputs                                                                                                                                                    | Intended contribution                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Foundation stewardship; reusable digital infrastructure; team knowledge; local volunteers; community and stakeholder expertise | Co-design workflows; configure team presence; train administrators; provide maintenance and support; coordinate agreed partnerships; review governance and safeguarding needs | Team administrators trained; agreed pages and records maintained; support requests tracked; stakeholder responsibilities documented; handovers and reviews completed | Stronger local administrative capability, more consistent communication, better continuity and more deliberate stakeholder coordination |

The final column is an **intended contribution**, not an impact claim. The programme should test whether the outputs are useful to teams and adjust the service accordingly. It should not attribute changes in participation, wellbeing, performance or community resilience to the website without a credible evaluation method.

### Value proposition

- **For teams:** useful infrastructure, practical training, an adult-controlled digital presence, and clear support without surrendering local decisions.
- **For Unami Foundation:** a repeatable way to steward support, coordinate stakeholders, document service commitments and learn across pilots.
- **For stakeholders:** an understandable point of coordination and a clearer picture of agreed needs, responsibilities and approved updates.
- **For communities:** a service intended to strengthen locally directed sport, with accessible processes and explicit care around young people’s information.

## 3. Operating principles

1. **Community authority:** a participating team governs its own affairs under its own lawful arrangements. The Foundation is an operating partner, not a substitute committee.
2. **Consent and transparency:** teams know what is collected, who can access it, what is public, why it is used and how it can be corrected or removed.
3. **Safeguarding before promotion:** visibility is not the default for youth profiles, photographs, contact details or fixture travel information.
4. **Capability over dependency:** training, documentation and administrator handover are part of the service, not optional extras.
5. **Support is a commitment:** explain what is donated, who maintains it, expected response routes, service boundaries and what happens when resources change.
6. **No invented evidence:** distinguish verified facts, team-approved information, working assumptions and fictional demonstration content.
7. **Appropriate technology:** choose a solution that fits volunteer time, device access, connectivity, accessibility needs and skills—not the other way around.
8. **Fair and inclusive participation:** ask teams and community members who is missing from planning and whether the service is usable in local languages and circumstances.
9. **Data minimisation:** collect only information needed for an agreed purpose; avoid sensitive details that the service cannot responsibly protect.
10. **Portability and exit:** retain an orderly route to export content, transfer administration or end a partnership without holding a team’s identity hostage.
11. **Accountability:** document decisions, approvals, complaints, changes, conflicts of interest and partnership responsibilities.
12. **Sport-agnostic foundation, locally specific practice:** reuse the infrastructure where useful, but do not force one sport’s rules or workflow on another.

## 4. The partnership and separation of concerns

The operating model has three distinct but connected domains. The separation is a product principle and must be reflected in agreements, training, access configuration and day-to-day decisions.

### A. Team-controlled domain

The team should lead and approve:

- Its team name, identity, community story and approved public representation.
- Sporting decisions, coaching approach, team selection and participation arrangements.
- Local schedules, training plans and coordination.
- Who represents the team and who can approve publication on its behalf.
- Team membership and operational records, subject to safeguarding and privacy responsibilities.
- Whether a proposed Foundation change meets the team’s need.
- Which team-specific content should remain private, be corrected or be removed.
- Local governance, committee decisions, finance and member accountability.

“Team-controlled” does not mean every volunteer should automatically access every record. The club should nominate appropriate adult roles and review them periodically.

### B. Foundation-stewarded domain

Unami Foundation, through Unami Sports, should coordinate:

- Stewardship of shared digital infrastructure and reusable team templates.
- Onboarding, platform training, maintenance and technical support within stated capacity.
- Stakeholder and partner relationships where the team has agreed the Foundation should coordinate them.
- Shared programme identity, service documentation and cross-pilot learning.
- A transparent change-request process and Foundation-managed delivery decisions.
- Agreed reporting to funders or stakeholders, with minimised and appropriately aggregated information.
- Continuity planning for shared services, including funding changes, access handovers and platform transition.

The Foundation should not claim ownership of a team’s sporting authority or use team information for stakeholder promotion beyond agreed purposes.

### C. Jointly agreed domain

Some matters require explicit agreement, including:

- Pilot onboarding scope and success measures.
- The boundary between team content and Foundation programme reporting.
- Support hours, escalation and maintenance expectations.
- Customisations that affect shared infrastructure or have ongoing cost.
- Public use of team names, photos, quotes, logos and stories.
- Safeguarding processes and escalation contacts.
- Data-controller/processor responsibilities and retention arrangements, after appropriate assessment.
- Incident response, complaints, relationship review, transfer or exit.

### Decision-rights matrix

| Decision or activity                                           | Team                                                       | Unami Foundation / Unami Sports                   | Joint / external confirmation                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sporting direction, selection and day-to-day team coordination | Accountable; leads                                         | No sporting authority unless separately agreed    | Consult local participants as appropriate                               |
| Team identity and team-authored public content                 | Approves and owns its representation                       | Provides publishing tools and support             | Agree use of shared programme branding                                  |
| Foundation programme identity and stakeholder reporting        | Consulted where the team is represented                    | Accountable for its own claims and commitments    | Team approves identifiable team material                                |
| Platform hosting, maintenance and shared template              | Informed; reports needs                                    | Accountable within documented service scope       | Jointly review major changes and costs                                  |
| Team-specific workflow request                                 | Defines need and approves fitness for purpose              | Assesses, prioritises and delivers if agreed      | Record scope, approval and ongoing impact                               |
| Youth profile or image publication                             | Team approval plus consent workflow                        | Provides safe controls and publication support    | Guardian permission, youth assent and safeguarding review as applicable |
| Fixture public listing                                         | Confirms accuracy and authorises publication               | Supports publication; does not invent schedule    | Confirm applicable competition information and safeguarding             |
| Club finances, committee and constitution                      | Accountable under its own governance                       | May support capacity-building if asked            | Appropriate legal/financial advice where needed                         |
| Stakeholder approach                                           | Leads local relationships unless delegated                 | Coordinates agreed Foundation-level relationships | Obtain permission before naming or implying endorsement                 |
| Access to Sanity and sensitive records                         | Nominated adult roles only                                 | Nominated platform stewards only                  | Configure actual permissions; workflow labels alone do not enforce this |
| Complaint or safeguarding concern                              | Use agreed reporting route; do not investigate beyond role | Escalate and support within role                  | Use competent, current professional/statutory channels                  |

This table describes the **intended model**, not a signed agreement, implemented role policy or technical guarantee.

## 5. Service catalogue

The “Sports Operating Partner” offer should be described as a bundle of bounded services rather than as software alone.

### 5.1 Donated digital infrastructure

Potential capabilities include:

- A team-branded public information site using an approved content structure.
- A managed content-management environment for authorised adult editors.
- Pages for the team, community, development approach, fixtures, contact and governance.
- Structured records for teams, players, fixtures, stakeholders, governance documents and customisation requests.
- Reusable templates for different sports, age groups and community contexts.
- Image, metadata and navigation management.

Infrastructure is only “donated” within the scope agreed for the pilot. Hosting, paid services, domains, connectivity, enhancements and long-term support should be itemised and reviewed rather than assumed costless.

### 5.2 Onboarding and platform training

Training should be task-based and accessible. A pilot should cover:

- Signing in securely and understanding authorised roles.
- Editing a team page, navigation and contact route.
- Drafting, previewing, approving and publishing content.
- Adding images only with appropriate rights, alt text and consent.
- Correcting or withdrawing information.
- Creating and updating fixtures without exposing unsafe details.
- Raising a support request and understanding escalation routes.
- Handover when an administrator leaves.

Where possible, train at least two team-nominated adults, use low-bandwidth/printable guides, and confirm understanding by having trainees perform common tasks—not by counting attendance alone.

### 5.3 Maintenance and technical support

The Foundation should state what it can reasonably maintain, how requests are logged, what is out of scope, who handles urgent incidents, and what response expectations apply. Do not publish a service-level promise before staffing and funding support it.

### 5.4 Stakeholder coordination

The Foundation can steward programme-level engagement, introductions and reporting. A stakeholder record is not evidence of a partnership. Obtain written permission before publishing an organisation’s name, logo, quote, endorsement, funding or contribution.

### 5.5 Customisation

Customisation should begin with the team’s need and then be assessed for:

- Team benefit and accessibility.
- Safeguarding and privacy impact.
- Whether the change belongs in a reusable template or a team-specific configuration.
- Development and maintenance effort.
- Whether other pilot teams will inherit the change.
- Ownership of content, assets and ongoing operation.
- Team approval, Foundation delivery approval and review date.

## 6. Digital infrastructure: what exists and how it fits

### 6.1 Current technical shape

The repository contains a **Next.js App Router website** with **Sanity Studio** embedded under `/admin`. Published page content is fetched from Sanity and composed from reusable modules. The project uses Sanity’s live-content tooling, typed GROQ queries and generated schema types. The CMS is the content and structured-record layer; Next.js renders the public website.

The current system is a useful content and workflow foundation. It is **not currently a complete club-management system**: it does not implement registration and eligibility management, payments, attendance, messaging, scheduling automation, safeguarding case management, protected file storage, finance/accounting, mobile offline sync, or enforce team-versus-Foundation permissions merely from the ownership labels.

### 6.2 Content and module capabilities

The existing page-building toolkit includes:

- Cover and split heroes; prose and callouts.
- Card, statistic, step, accordion and tabbed-content lists.
- Image galleries, person, quote and logo lists.
- Search and blog-related modules.
- Custom HTML/CSS for carefully governed brand/layout extensions.
- Shared navigation, site branding, announcements and global page modules.

These modules let editors assemble a site without creating a new code module for every page. A custom visual module or workflow still requires engineering work and review.

### 6.3 New reusable sports schemas

The CMS schema now includes:

| Document type          | Intended use                                                                                                    | Important boundary                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `sports.team`          | Team identity, sport, community, squad band, status, demo marker and operational owner                          | The owner field is descriptive; it does not restrict access.                                                                                                                                 |
| `sports.player`        | A minimal player-facing profile related to a team                                                               | Publication defaults off; broad squad only; no date of birth field; publication validation requires approved guardian consent. This is not a complete safeguarding or consent-record system. |
| `sports.fixture`       | Competition, opponent, date/time, venue, result and report                                                      | Public listing defaults off; review youth travel/location details before publication.                                                                                                        |
| `sports.stakeholder`   | Foundation/team relationship lead, support areas and public-recognition approval                                | A record does not prove the relationship or authorise public acknowledgement.                                                                                                                |
| `sports.customization` | Team need, sport/area, team decision, Foundation delivery status and review                                     | A tracked workflow record, not an automated service desk or permission rule.                                                                                                                 |
| `sports.governance`    | Draft constitution, safeguarding, finance, privacy and related material with owner/status/version/review fields | Draft status is not organisational approval; content must be reviewed before adoption.                                                                                                       |

Schemas constrain some field choices and defaults. They do not by themselves ensure correct CMS roles, publication workflows, consent evidence, secure incident handling or compliance.

### 6.4 Current demo data and public presentation

The Sanity dataset has been populated with:

- Ten published, populated demo pages: home, Unami Stars, operating model, development, fixtures, governance, get involved, contact, accessibility and a no-index 404 page.
- One site singleton and connected header/footer/utility navigation.
- A custom wordmark and social-preview image.
- Global brand styling and a public announcement that the team is fictional.
- One demo team, six fictional player examples, one explicitly unscheduled fixture, one Foundation stakeholder record and one proposed customisation example.
- Three governance starters kept as unpublished CMS drafts.

Player examples and the sample fixture are not publicly listed. Contact routes, images of real young people, verified affiliation and local facts still need authorised input before public launch. The records and prose are demonstration content, not proof of operations.

### 6.5 Important technical control gap

The current sports schemas include `operationalOwner`, `relationshipLead`, `owner`, `teamApproval`, `guardianConsent` and public-listing booleans. These are **workflow and editorial fields**, not access-control enforcement. Anyone with sufficient Sanity project access may be able to see or change records regardless of the field value.

Before a real pilot stores non-public personal or safeguarding information:

1. Define the minimum information the CMS should hold; keep highly sensitive case details out of the general CMS unless a separately assessed system is approved.
2. Configure and test Sanity roles, least privilege, publishing rights and account offboarding.
3. Separate public content from private operational data where practical.
4. Confirm organisational responsibilities for personal information and processing.
5. Create incident, correction, deletion and access-review procedures.
6. Test public GROQ queries to ensure private and demo records cannot leak.
7. Assess whether Sanity is appropriate for every proposed record category.

## 7. Pilot definition: Unami Stars

### 7.1 Pilot status

Unami Stars is a **fictional demonstrator** created to make the operating model tangible for teams, community stakeholders and potential supporters. The CMS currently uses a village spelling based on the supplied brief; confirm the community’s preferred spelling, geographic details and permission to use the name before launch.

Do not imply that:

- Unami Stars is registered as a legal entity or affiliated with a league or football association.
- A named cohort, coach, committee, stakeholder or sponsor exists.
- A fixture, competition, venue or result is confirmed.
- The team has adopted the draft governance documents.
- A training, maintenance, funding or other service has been delivered unless it has.

### 7.2 Pilot objectives

The pilot should test the service model—not just whether the pages look finished.

1. Can team-nominated adults update approved public information after training?
2. Can the team explain which decisions it controls and which the Foundation coordinates?
3. Is the support route clear and realistic for volunteers?
4. Can the team operate through likely connectivity, device and volunteer constraints?
5. Are safeguarding and publication decisions understood and practised?
6. Do stakeholders understand that they are supporting a team-led relationship rather than taking over the club?
7. Is the recurring cost and maintenance workload understood by both sides?
8. Can another sport or team reuse the infrastructure without carrying forward football-specific assumptions?

### 7.3 Pilot stages and exit criteria

| Stage                                | Activities                                                                                                                            | Exit evidence                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0. Verify and listen                 | Confirm team/community identity, local priorities, adult contacts, authority to participate and preferred language/access needs       | Named adult team representatives; recorded consent to pilot scope; verified facts separated from demo material |
| 1. Agree boundaries                  | Document roles, publication approvals, data responsibilities, support scope, costs, complaints and exit/transfer                      | Signed or otherwise formally approved pilot agreement; unresolved legal and safeguarding questions assigned    |
| 2. Safeguarding and access readiness | Name a competent safeguarding lead; agree reporting routes; assess data collection; configure accounts and roles                      | Tested least-privilege permissions; no unnecessary child data; approved publication checklist                  |
| 3. Configure                         | Confirm site identity, pages, contacts, team records and any necessary customisation                                                  | Team approves preview; demo markers removed only where facts are verified; rollback path recorded              |
| 4. Train                             | Train at least two nominated adult administrators where feasible; practise common edits, publishing, incident escalation and handover | Administrators complete practical tasks; accessible guide and support route available                          |
| 5. Limited launch                    | Publish only approved pages and information; monitor errors and support requests                                                      | Team confirms public content; no unresolved high-risk privacy/safeguarding issues                              |
| 6. Review and decide                 | Gather team feedback, support burden, stakeholder feedback and cost/continuity evidence                                               | Continue, change, pause or exit decision documented with owners and dates                                      |

### 7.4 Proportionate pilot measures

Select measures with the team before the pilot begins. Potential operational indicators include:

- Number of team-nominated adults trained and able to complete agreed tasks.
- Percentage of public pages with team approval and current review date.
- Support requests by category and time to acknowledgement/resolution, if consistently recorded.
- Number of successful handovers or account-access reviews.
- Number of content corrections or takedown requests completed within the agreed process.
- Team-reported confidence and usefulness, collected without unnecessary personal details.
- Cost and staff/volunteer time required to establish and maintain the service.
- Stakeholder commitments documented versus commitments fulfilled.

Do not turn small pilot counts into claims about youth outcomes, regional reach or social impact. Report sample size, timeframe, limitations and what the measure actually shows.

## 8. Governance and South African context

This section identifies governance topics for local review; it does not determine legal status or supply a compliant constitution.

### 8.1 Unami Foundation legal identity

Before describing Unami Foundation as an **NPC**, confirm its registered name and status against its current legal records and applicable South African records. Non-profit purpose, NPC company form, tax treatment, public-benefit status and fundraising permissions are distinct matters; do not conflate them. Have the Foundation’s responsible leadership or qualified South African advisers confirm the correct public description.

If the Foundation operates on a social-enterprise model, explain that as the **operating and sustainability approach** while keeping its verified legal form explicit. “Social enterprise” does not replace the organisation’s registration or constitutional obligations.

### 8.2 Participating team status

A team may be an informal community group, a club, an association, a non-profit entity or another structure. Do not assume each pilot team is incorporated or governed the same way. For each team, confirm:

- Which body or members have authority to approve participation and public representation.
- Whether the team has a constitution or other governing instrument.
- Who is authorised to make decisions and handle funds.
- What local league, district, provincial or national football requirements apply.
- Whether insurance, registration, venue permission, coaching credentials or other requirements need confirmation.

### 8.3 Constitution starter

The CMS draft includes topics such as name and purpose; membership; committee roles; meetings and decisions; finances and assets; safeguarding and conduct; association requirements; complaints; amendments; and closure. Each team must adapt and approve its own governing document through its valid process. A template is not an adopted constitution.

### 8.4 Youth safeguarding and information

Youth sport needs a practical, locally reviewed safeguarding framework covering:

- A named safeguarding lead and a trusted reporting route.
- Adult boundaries, supervision, changing arrangements and transport.
- Responding to concerns and escalating appropriately.
- Photography, publication, identity, location and withdrawal of consent.
- Access to, retention of and deletion of personal information.
- Volunteer/coach suitability and training, as appropriate to the organisation and current requirements.
- A distinction between ordinary team administration and sensitive safeguarding case information.

Assess applicable South African child-protection, privacy and sport requirements with competent advisers and relevant authorities. Do not rely on a CMS checkbox as evidence that consent was adequately obtained or that a process is compliant.

### 8.5 Football governance

The team and its advisers should confirm current requirements directly with the relevant local competition and football structures, including any applicable district/provincial bodies and SAFA. Rules and affiliation pathways can vary and change. This document does not certify eligibility or affiliation.

## 9. Governance of data, publishing and consent

### 9.1 Classify information before collecting it

| Information class                  | Example                                                               | Default handling                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public and approved                | Team mission, public contact inbox, approved public venue             | Publish after team approval and factual review                                                                                                 |
| Internal operational               | Draft schedules, volunteer task notes, unpublished content            | Restrict to nominated adult roles; do not assume drafts are private from all Studio users                                                      |
| Personal information               | Adult contact name, player profile                                    | Minimise, explain purpose, limit access and retention                                                                                          |
| Youth-identifying content          | Child name, image, school, detailed routine or fixture travel pattern | Keep unpublished unless a justified purpose and appropriate consent/assent and safeguarding review are documented                              |
| Sensitive safeguarding information | Allegations, case notes, health or safety concerns                    | Do not store in general content records without a separate suitability, access and retention assessment; use an appropriate restricted process |
| Demonstration content              | Fictional player, sample fixture, draft partner relationship          | Mark clearly as demo; never represent as verified; do not mix with real records without review                                                 |

### 9.2 Publication workflow

1. Author drafts the content and labels its source: verified, team-provided, Foundation-provided, or illustrative.
2. Editor checks accuracy, accessibility, image rights, privacy and safeguarding considerations.
3. Team approver signs off identifiable team content and fixture/public-facing information.
4. Foundation approver signs off Foundation claims, stakeholder representation and shared programme assets.
5. Publish only after required approvals; record approver and review date using an agreed workflow.
6. Provide a route to request correction, takedown, withdrawal or deletion.
7. Review public content periodically and when circumstances change.

Sanity draft/published status and the present schema fields do not yet implement this full approval audit trail. Establish the human process and assess whether technical workflow or role customisation is needed.

### 9.3 Consent is not a single checkbox

For a young person’s identifiable image or profile, the operating process should consider purpose, audience, context, guardian permission, age-appropriate assent, safeguarding review, validity period and withdrawal. Keep evidence in a suitably restricted record system; do not upload sensitive consent documents into a broadly accessible CMS without a documented assessment.

## 10. Sustainability and resilience

### 10.1 Sustainability dimensions

- **Financial:** identify recurring costs, in-kind support, funding dependencies and what happens when a grant ends.
- **Operational:** document service ownership, support capacity, backups, account recovery and handover.
- **Human:** reduce single-volunteer dependency; train backups; respect volunteer time.
- **Technical:** keep dependencies and hosting supportable; use exportable content; document integrations and recovery.
- **Community:** maintain team authority and inclusive decision-making; avoid imposing unnecessary administration.
- **Environmental and access practicality:** consider travel, low-bandwidth usage, device longevity and practical alternatives where digital-only service would exclude people.
- **Institutional:** preserve decision records, agreements, policies and role accountability.

### 10.2 Minimum continuity plan

For each pilot, document:

- The team’s nominated primary and backup administrators.
- Foundation service owner and backup contact route.
- What accounts, domains, hosting and data the Foundation manages.
- What happens if the Foundation cannot continue support.
- How the team exports its content and approved media.
- How credentials are rotated and access removed during handover.
- How backups and restoration are tested.
- What costs may arise and who approves them.
- How the team can pause or exit without losing its identity or content.

### 10.3 Resilience scenarios to rehearse

- The only trained editor is unavailable.
- Connectivity is weak before an event.
- A phone or account is lost or compromised.
- A fixture is postponed or a venue detail should be withdrawn.
- A young person or guardian withdraws consent.
- A stakeholder withdraws a logo or acknowledgement.
- A volunteer publishes inaccurate or unsafe information.
- Hosting, a vendor, funding or Foundation capacity becomes unavailable.
- A team changes its name, leadership, sport, or relationship to the pilot.

## 11. Sport-specific customisation strategy

The reusable core should describe an **organisation, participants, events, approved public information, relationships and governance**. Sport-specific terminology and workflows should be configured or added deliberately.

| Sport                           | Possible extension areas to validate with teams                                                  |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Football / soccer               | Squad bands, fixtures, competition, results, training and affiliation workflow                   |
| Rugby                           | Age-grade safety, match-day roles, referee/competition details and appropriate contact workflows |
| Basketball                      | Squad/age group, fixtures, roster roles, results and facility access                             |
| Tennis                          | Individual or pair participation, court availability, draws, coaching and match results          |
| Netball                         | Squad roles, fixtures, venue and competition information                                         |
| Cricket                         | Match format, innings/result summary, training and equipment needs                               |
| Athletics                       | Event disciplines, meet schedules, participation categories and results                          |
| Multi-sport community programme | Shared participant/guardian safeguards with distinct sport-specific activities                   |

These are prompts, not requirements. Do not store medical, eligibility or sensitive performance information merely because a sport might use it. Confirm why the information is needed, who may see it and how long it must be retained before adding fields.

### Customisation governance

Every requested customisation should have:

- A team-stated problem and intended user.
- A team decision and named adult approver.
- A Foundation delivery owner.
- Safeguarding, privacy, accessibility and security considerations.
- A decision: shared feature, local configuration, manual workaround, defer or decline.
- Estimate of implementation and recurring maintenance burden.
- A release, review and rollback plan.
- A note on whether other teams inherit the change.

Prefer configuration and content modules for differences in story, brand and page composition. Add schema or code only when a real workflow need cannot be safely met by existing configuration.

## 12. Roles and capability model

### Suggested role concepts

- **Foundation programme steward:** owns programme scope, partnership standards, stakeholder coordination and sustainability decisions.
- **Foundation technical steward:** administers infrastructure, maintenance, security updates, backups and technical support.
- **Foundation content approver:** approves Foundation claims and shared programme assets.
- **Team committee/authorised representative:** approves team identity, pilot participation, local governance and team publications.
- **Team administrator/editor:** maintains agreed team records and public content.
- **Coach or activity lead:** contributes sporting information and validates schedules within agreed authority.
- **Safeguarding lead:** owns the safeguarding process and escalation route within the organisation’s mandate.
- **Guardian and young participant:** provide permission/assent where required and can raise correction, consent or takedown concerns through a safe route.
- **Stakeholder or supporter:** contributes only under clear, agreed terms and does not acquire control of the team.

One person may hold more than one function in a small community organisation, but approval, publishing and financial controls should be separated where practical. The system should make conflicts and backups visible rather than pretending a small team has a large staff structure.

### Capability ladder

1. **Observe:** Foundation demonstrates the workflow and listens.
2. **Practise together:** team administrators perform tasks with support.
3. **Operate locally:** team handles routine updates and knows how to get help.
4. **Handover confidently:** a backup administrator can maintain continuity.
5. **Improve together:** team proposes changes; Foundation evaluates shared infrastructure impact.
6. **Transition or replicate:** team can export/transition its records; Foundation can onboard another team from documented practice.

Progress should be judged by demonstrated capability and team confidence, not by how many CMS fields are populated.

## 14. Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Foundation readiness

- Verify legal identity, governance authority, insurance and public-facing description.
- Confirm technical and service owners, budget, recurring costs and capacity.
- Define initial data-protection, account-security and incident processes.
- Confirm who approves public representations of the Foundation.

- Identify a real team and its authorised representatives.
- Listen to players, guardians, coaches and relevant community voices safely.
- Agree boundaries, support offer, data responsibilities, success measures, duration and exit.
- Confirm relevant sport structures and local requirements with appropriate sources.

### Phase 2 — Safety and infrastructure controls

- Configure least-privilege Studio roles and publishing responsibilities.
- Test public queries for unintended disclosure.
- Finalise consent, image, correction, complaint, incident and retention procedures.
- Validate backup, recovery, account handover and export.
- Confirm accessibility and low-connectivity needs.

### Phase 3 — Configure and train

- Replace demo identity only with verified, authorised content.
- Add approved contacts, team information and suitable images.
- Train primary and backup team editors using realistic tasks.
- Record support routes and Foundation escalation boundaries.

### Phase 4 — Limited launch and learning

- Publish only approved pages and appropriate information.
- Monitor support burden, user feedback, page accuracy and privacy requests.
- Hold regular team-led reviews; remove content that is no longer needed.
- Report results honestly, distinguishing outputs from outcomes.

### Phase 5 — Adapt, sustain or exit

- Decide with the team whether to continue, change, expand or stop.
- Fund maintenance and support explicitly; do not assume recurring services are covered.
- Reuse proven templates across another sport only after local discovery and safety review.
- Provide a supported transition and data export if the partnership ends.

## 16. Product management decisions still required

These are deliberate decisions for Foundation and pilot-team leadership; this document does not silently decide them:

1. **Legal identity:** What exact registered entity and public designation should be used for Unami Foundation?
2. **Programme mandate:** What services are guaranteed, optional, time-limited or dependent on funding?
3. **Pilot duration:** What is the pilot term and who can renew, pause or end it?
4. **Support capacity:** Who responds, during which hours, through what channel, and with what realistic expectations?
5. **Data stewardship:** Which party determines purposes and means for each class of information, and what written arrangements are needed?
6. **Account separation:** Does each team need an isolated Sanity project/dataset, dataset-level access, or shared editorial space with carefully scoped roles? Evaluate privacy, cost and operating capacity before choosing.
7. **Approval process:** Who can draft, approve and publish Foundation, team and youth-related content?
8. **Safeguarding:** Who is the competent lead, what training and referral arrangements exist, and which data should never be stored in this CMS?
9. **Funding and costs:** Which recurring infrastructure and support costs are paid by the Foundation, a funder, the team or another agreed source?
10. **Impact learning:** Which modest, ethical indicators will teams accept and what will not be collected?
11. **Replication:** What minimum evidence is required before adapting the model to another community or sport?
12. **Constitutional relationship:** How does the Foundation’s own governing document authorise the programme, donations, partnerships and stewardship?

## Glossary

- **Community sports operating partnership (CSOP):** the proposed service relationship in which a community team retains its authority while a partner supplies agreed operational and infrastructure support.
- **Digital infrastructure:** shared technical assets—site, CMS, schemas, hosting and support processes—that enable the service.
- **Operating partner:** an organisation that performs agreed ongoing coordination and support; not automatically a governing body or owner.
- **Participating team:** a community sports organisation taking part in an agreed pilot or programme.
- **Stewardship:** responsible care of shared resources, relationships, information and continuity.
- **Demonstration record:** fictional or sample CMS data clearly labelled as non-factual and excluded from unintended public presentation.
- **Social-enterprise model:** an approach to sustaining social-purpose activity through organised operations and potentially earned or blended resources; not a legal designation by itself.
- **NPC:** South African non-profit company legal form; use only when the organisation’s actual registration supports the claim.
- **Publication approval:** explicit review by the person/organisation authorised to approve a public representation; not equivalent to consent, legal compliance or access control.
- **Least privilege:** provide each user only the system access required for their authorised role.
